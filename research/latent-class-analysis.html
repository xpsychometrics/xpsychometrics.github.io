Latent Class Analysis

Uncovering hidden subpopulations in psychological and educational data

← All Research Areas

Overview

Latent Class Analysis (LCA) is a model-based clustering approach that identifies discrete subgroups (latent classes) within a population based on patterns of observed responses — without requiring any predefined groupings.

Unlike continuous latent variable models such as factor analysis or IRT, LCA assumes the underlying construct is categorical. This makes it particularly useful for identifying qualitatively distinct profiles — for example, different types of learners, responders, or error patterns.

Our work bridges LCA with other psychometric frameworks, including cognitive diagnosis models (which are themselves a constrained form of LCA), and applies LCA to reveal meaningful structure in educational and psychological data.

Key Research Themes

Learner Profiling

Identifying distinct learner subgroups based on patterns of skill mastery, error tendencies, or behavioral engagement.

Model Selection

Developing principled approaches for determining the number of latent classes and evaluating model fit in complex data settings.

CDM as Constrained LCA

Exploiting the formal connection between CDMs and LCA to develop more general and flexible diagnostic models.

Mixture Models

Extending LCA to mixture IRT and factor analytic models to allow for both continuous and categorical latent structures.

LCA vs. Factor Analysis

LCA: Latent variable is categorical; identifies discrete subgroups.

Factor Analysis: Latent variable is continuous; identifies common dimensions.

Mixture models combine both, allowing for nuanced representations of populations.
